COVARIANCE VS VARIANCE

1. Introduction

Variance and covariance are important concepts in statistics, probability, and machine learning. Both describe how data values vary, but they answer different questions.

Variance tells us how much a single variable spreads around its mean.

Covariance tells us how two variables change together.

------------------------------------------------------------

2. VARIANCE

Variance measures the amount of spread or dispersion in a single random variable.

In simple words:

Variance answers:
"How far are the values of one variable spread from its average?"

For a random variable X:

Var(X) = E[(X - E[X])^2]

Another commonly used form is:

Var(X) = E[X^2] - (E[X])^2

For a sample, variance is commonly calculated as:

s² = Σ(xᵢ - x̄)² / (n - 1)

Where:
- xᵢ = individual data value
- x̄ = sample mean
- n = number of observations

Example:

Suppose the marks are:

10, 20, 30

Mean = (10 + 20 + 30) / 3 = 20

The values are 10 away from the mean on both sides, so there is some amount of spread. Variance converts these deviations into squared values and averages them.

Important point:

Variance is always non-negative because deviations are squared.

A variance of 0 means all values are exactly the same.

------------------------------------------------------------

3. COVARIANCE

Covariance measures how two variables change together.

In simple words:

Covariance answers:
"When one variable changes, does the other variable tend to change in the same direction or in the opposite direction?"

For two random variables X and Y:

Cov(X,Y) = E[(X - E[X])(Y - E[Y])]

For a sample:

Cov(X,Y) = Σ(xᵢ - x̄)(yᵢ - ȳ) / (n - 1)

There are three important cases:

Positive covariance:
When X increases, Y tends to increase.

Negative covariance:
When X increases, Y tends to decrease.

Covariance close to zero:
There is little or no linear relationship between the variables.

Example:

Consider:

Study Hours: 1, 2, 3
Marks:       40, 60, 80

As study hours increase, marks also increase. Therefore, the covariance between study hours and marks is positive.

Another example:

Temperature: 10, 20, 30
Jacket Sales: 80, 50, 20

As temperature increases, jacket sales decrease. Therefore, covariance is negative.

------------------------------------------------------------

4. MAIN DIFFERENCE

Variance deals with ONE variable.

Covariance deals with TWO variables.

Variance:
"How much does X vary?"

Covariance:
"How do X and Y vary together?"

------------------------------------------------------------

5. SIMPLE COMPARISON

Variance:
- Number of variables: 1
- Measures: Spread of one variable
- Can be positive or zero
- Cannot be negative
- Units are squared
- Example: Variance of students' marks

Covariance:
- Number of variables: 2
- Measures: Direction of joint variation
- Can be positive, negative, or zero
- Units are the product of the two variables' units
- Example: Covariance between study hours and marks

------------------------------------------------------------

6. RELATIONSHIP BETWEEN VARIANCE AND COVARIANCE

Variance is actually a special case of covariance.

If we take X and X itself:

Cov(X, X) = Var(X)

Therefore:

Var(X) = Cov(X, X)

This is an important relationship to remember.

------------------------------------------------------------

7. ROLE IN MACHINE LEARNING

Variance is commonly used to understand how much a feature varies.

For example:

Feature: Age
Values: 18, 19, 20, 21, 22

Variance tells us how much the ages differ from their average.

Covariance is useful when studying relationships between features.

For example:

Feature 1: Hours studied
Feature 2: Exam marks

A positive covariance suggests that these two variables tend to increase together.

Covariance is also used in constructing a covariance matrix, which is important in techniques such as Principal Component Analysis (PCA).

------------------------------------------------------------

8. COVARIANCE MATRIX

When we have multiple variables, we can calculate covariance between every pair of variables.

For example, for variables X and Y:

        X             Y

X     Var(X)       Cov(X,Y)

Y     Cov(Y,X)     Var(Y)

The diagonal contains variances.

The off-diagonal elements contain covariances.

For example:

[ Var(X)      Cov(X,Y) ]
[ Cov(Y,X)    Var(Y)   ]

This type of matrix is called a covariance matrix.

------------------------------------------------------------

9. IMPORTANT LIMITATION OF COVARIANCE

The magnitude of covariance is difficult to compare across different datasets because it depends on the units of the variables.

For example, covariance between height measured in meters and weight measured in kilograms will have different numerical properties if the units are changed.

For this reason, correlation is often preferred when we want a standardized measure of linear relationship.

Correlation is related to covariance:

Correlation(X,Y) = Cov(X,Y) / (σX σY)

where σX and σY are the standard deviations of X and Y.

Correlation always lies between -1 and +1.

------------------------------------------------------------

10. EASY WAY TO REMEMBER

Variance:

ONE variable
       ↓
How much does it spread?

Covariance:

TWO variables
       ↓
How do they move together?

------------------------------------------------------------

11. FINAL SUMMARY

Variance measures the spread of a single variable around its mean. It is always non-negative and is expressed in squared units.

Covariance measures how two variables change together. It can be positive, negative, or zero. Positive covariance means the variables tend to move in the same direction, while negative covariance means they tend to move in opposite directions.

The key relationship is:

Var(X) = Cov(X, X)

Therefore, variance can be considered a special case of covariance.
